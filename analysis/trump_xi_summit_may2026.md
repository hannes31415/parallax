# Case Study 1: Trump–Xi Beijing Summit
**May 14–15, 2026**  
**Sources:** CNN (English); Al Jazeera (Arabic); Xinhua / People's Daily (Chinese)

---

## Methodology

Articles were retrieved in their source languages and analyzed across five dimensions: outcome framing, lexical choice on key referents, epistemic modality, agency and voice, and a composite structural divergence score. The goal here is not to identify which source is "biased" but to surface systematic structural differences in how the same event is encoded across languages and editorial traditions.

---

## 1. Outcome Framing

**CNN — "Failure / missed opportunity"**  
The summit is framed primarily through what didn't happen. "No substantive agreements," "failure to commit on Taiwan," "meetings ended without any substantive agreements announced on key issues." Trump's claims are immediately hedged against China's non-confirmation. The dominant lens is accountability journalism — measuring the gap between what was promised and what was delivered.

**Al Jazeera Arabic — "Symbolic victory for China"**  
Al Jazeera synthesizes US press criticism (directly citing Politico, Washington Post, Foreign Policy) but adds its own analytical layer: the summit was a Chinese soft-power win. Xi "managed the scene" (أدار المشهد), Beijing set the terms of parity. The frame is geopolitical theater, discussing who gained face, who conceded ground. Notably, AJ goes further than the American outlets it's quoting, asserting China's rise as settled fact rather than contested interpretation.

**Xinhua / People's Daily — "Historic partnership and shared stewardship"**  
Xi is presented as the architect of a new world order, asking "historical questions, world questions, people's questions" (历史之问、世界之问、人民之问) that only great-power leaders can answer together. The summit is not about what was achieved but what was affirmed. Cooperation language is most prominent in the text, and disagreement is recast as "manageable differences." A new bilateral doctrine is formally named: "China-US constructive strategic stable relationship" (中美建设性战略稳定关系).

## 2. Lexical Choice

Different labels are given to the same labels, highlighting ideological differences.

| Source | Label | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CNN | "Chinese leader" | Depersonalized, rank-neutral |
| Al Jazeera | "الرئيس الصيني" (the Chinese president) | Formal, equal rank to Trump |
| Xinhua | "国家主席" (State Chairman) | Title-first, constitutional weight, institutional authority |

**Trump's Taiwan signal:**
| Source | Framing | Notes |
|---|---|---|

## 3. Epistemic Modality

*How certain is the language? How are claims attributed?*

**CNN — low to medium certainty**  
Heavy attribution and hedging throughout. Nearly every Trump claim is immediately qualified:
- "Trump said… although China's foreign ministry did not confirm…"
- Scare quotes signal distancing: Trump said *"a lot of different problems were settled"*
- "Loomed large" — the Iran war is an atmospheric force, not an asserted fact

Key markers: *said, touted, did not confirm, loomed, although*

**Al Jazeera Arabic — medium certainty**  
Synthesizes US media criticism as established fact, then adds its own analytical certainty on top:
- "الصين باتت أكثر ثقة" — China *has become* more confident (completed state change, asserted as fact)
- "لم تعد بحاجة" — *no longer needs* (asserted, not hedged)
- "خلصت المجلة إلى أن" — the magazine *concluded that…* (cites sources, treats conclusions as reliable)

Key markers: *باتت (has become), خلص (concluded), يرى (sees/believes)*

**Xinhua — very high certainty, almost no hedging**  
This is the most linguistically distinctive feature across the three sources. The critical mechanism is the verb **"指出" (zhǐchū)**:

- "习近平指出" — Xi *points out* [that X is true]

In English journalism, the equivalent would be "said" or "stated" — neutral attribution verbs. But "points out" is a **factive verb**: it grammatically presupposes the truth of what follows. The contrast:

> *"He said the essence of trade relations is mutual benefit"* — leaves truth open  
> *"He pointed out that the essence of trade relations is mutual benefit"* — presupposes it's true

This is not a translation artifact. In the source Chinese, "指出" is systematically used for Xi's statements in state media, while "said/stated" equivalents are used for foreign leaders. It encodes epistemic authority at the grammatical level.

Additional Xinhua markers: *强调 (emphasizes), 本质是 (the essence is), 致力于 (is committed to)*

## 4. Agency and Voice

*Who does things vs. who has things done to them?*

**CNN:**  
- Trump is the grammatical subject of most sentences, but frequently shown *failing* to act
- China "touted," "did not confirm" — reactive and withholding
- "The Iran war loomed large" — the war itself is the agent, a force acting on the summit from outside

**Al Jazeera Arabic:**  
- Xi is the most active strategic agent: "استغل القمة" — *exploited* the summit (strong volition verb)
- China as historical actor: "باتت أكثر ثقة" — *has become* more confident
- Trump is largely defined by inaction: "لم يلتزم" (did not commit) — passive against Xi's strategy

**Xinhua:**  
- Xi is the subject of virtually every sentence, always with volition verbs: 指出, 强调, 愿 (wishes/intends)
- Trump appears as Xi's interlocutor: "我愿同特朗普总统" — "I wish [to navigate with] President Trump" — secondary, almost a prop
- Xi is positioned as answering not just Trump but *history itself*: "历史之问、世界之问、人民之问" — Xi's agency is civilizational, not merely political

**Pattern across all three:** All three sources agree Xi has more agency than Trump, but they frame it differently. CNN: Trump failed to act. Al Jazeera: Xi strategically exploited the moment. Xinhua: Xi is the natural author of history.

---

## 5. Structural Divergence Summary

| Dimension | Score (1–5) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome framing | 5 | Failure vs. undeclared truce vs. triumph |
| Taiwan treatment | 5 | Policy gap vs. sensitivity vs. civilizational threat |
| Epistemic certainty | 4.5 | CNN hedges everything; Xinhua asserts everything |
| Lexical loading | 4 | Especially on Taiwan and bilateral relationship labels |
| Agency attribution | 3 | All agree Xi > Trump, but reasons differ |

---

## Key Finding

The most technically significant result from this case study is the **factive verb divergence**: Xinhua's systematic use of "指出" (points out) vs. CNN's "said/touted" represents a *grammatically encodable* difference in epistemic commitment. This is less interpretive, and more a verifiable property of the source-language text that automated analysis can detect at scale. It suggests that cross-lingual framing analysis operating only on translated text is systematically missing the most ideologically loaded signals.
